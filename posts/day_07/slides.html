Slide 2 — What clean data means
Clean data is more than 'no missing values'
1
Accurate: entries reflect reality and are not distorted by bad labels or bad conversions.
2
Consistent: units, formats, and categories follow the same rules across every source.
3
Complete: the fields your model needs are present, or missing data is handled intentionally.
4
Feature-ready: variables are encoded, scaled, and aligned with the prediction task.
The actual definition
Clean data is trustworthy, structured, and purpose-built for the ML problem — not just pretty rows in a spreadsheet.

Slide 4 — Skills recruiters see
What hiring teams actually look for
What stands out
SQL, ETL pipelines, data validation checks, feature engineering, and experience cleaning messy real-world datasets.
What gets missed
Data contracts, schema drift handling, reproducible preprocessing, error logging, and documenting assumptions.
The recruiter gap
Many candidates talk about models. Few can prove they can make data stable enough to keep a model working in production.

Slide 5 — What to showcase
Show the process, not just the final model
1
Pipeline walkthrough: describe how raw data became the training dataset.
2
Before/after samples: compare messy input to the cleaned dataset used for the model.
3
Validation checks: highlight duplicates, schema issues, and drift caught before training.
4
Business impact: explain why the cleanup mattered for accuracy, reliability, or deployment.
Final takeaway
Answer data prep questions with a concrete pipeline and a cleanup example, not just a model name.
