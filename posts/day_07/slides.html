Slide 1 — Data Lifecycle
From raw source to model-ready input
Raw source
Logs, spreadsheets, APIs, sensors — data arrives messy, incomplete, and inconsistent.
Ingest + validation
Collect, standardize, and verify values before anything else can be trusted.
Clean + enrich
Fix duplicates, normalize formats, fill gaps, and derive features that matter.
Feature-ready
Transform columns into model inputs and make sure every row is complete and consistent.
Why this matters
A model trained on garbage data learns garbage patterns. Clean data is the foundation of accuracy, explainability, and production stability.
Slide 2 — What clean data means
Clean data is not just "no missing values"
1
Accurate: values reflect reality, not noise or mislabeled entries.
2
Consistent: formats, units, and categories follow the same rules across every row.
3
Complete: required fields are filled, or missingness is intentionally handled.
4
Feature-ready: variables are engineered, encoded, and scaled for the model you will use.
The full definition
Clean data is trustworthy, structured, and purpose-built for the ML problem — not just pretty-looking rows in a spreadsheet.
Slide 3 — Before / after example
Turn noisy source data into a model-ready table
Before
Date values in mixed formats, missing city names, inconsistent category labels, and duplicate rows from repeated uploads.
After
Standardized dates, filled missing values, normalized categories, deduplicated rows, and a clean target variable ready for training.
What changed
This is the real work: removing ambiguity, enforcing structure, and making sure the algorithm sees consistent signals instead of random noise.
Slide 4 — Skills recruiters see
The data prep strengths that stand out
What recruiters want
SQL + ETL pipelines, data cleaning tools, feature engineering, validation checks, and experience with messy real-world datasets.
What often gets missed
Data contracts, schema drift handling, reproducible preprocessing, error logging, and annotation of assumptions.
The gap
Many candidates talk about models. Few show they can make data reliable in production — and that difference is what hiring teams notice.
Slide 5 — What to showcase
Your data prep portfolio should prove process, not just output
1
Pipeline walkthrough: share the steps, tools, and decisions from raw source to training dataset.
2
Before/after samples: show the messy input and the clean table the model actually used.
3
Validation checks: demonstrate how you caught bad values, duplicates, and drift before training began.
4
Business impact: explain why the cleanup mattered for accuracy, reliability, or onboarding a new model.
Final note
If your next interview asks "How do you prepare data?", answer with a concrete pipeline, a cleanup example, and a clear business result.
